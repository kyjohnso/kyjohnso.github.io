🌳 Natural timbers
Hardwoods and softwoods — where they come from, what they're like, what they're used for

🌲 Where timber comes from

  • Timber comes from trees. Trees must grow to full maturity — between 25 and 100 years — before they can be cut down for wood.
  • There are two groups of natural timber: hardwoods and softwoods. The third type of timber material is manufactured board (see the next section).
⚠️ Watch out! "Hardwood" and "softwood" describe the type of tree, not how hard the wood feels. Balsa is a hardwood, but it is one of the softest woods there is!

🍂 Hardwoods

  • Come from deciduous trees — large, flat leaves that fall in autumn.
  • Most grow slowly (balsa is a fast-growing exception), are not easily sourced, and are expensive.
  • Examples: ash, beech, mahogany, oak, balsa.

🌲 Softwoods

  • Come from coniferous trees — needles and cones; most are evergreen all year. (Larch is an exception — it drops its needles in autumn.)
  • Grow faster, so they're cheaper and considered a sustainable material.
  • Examples: larch, pine, spruce.

What is the difference between a hardwood and a softwood?
Hardwoods come from deciduous trees (broad leaves that fall in autumn) — slow-growing and expensive. Softwoods come from coniferous trees (needles, evergreen) — faster-growing, cheaper and more sustainable.
Balsa is very soft. Is it a hardwood or a softwood?
A hardwood — because it comes from a broad-leaved (deciduous-type) tree, not a conifer. Hardwood/softwood is about the type of tree, not how hard the wood is.
Which wood would you choose for sports equipment, and why?
Ash — it is tough and flexible, and good for steam bending.
Why are softwoods called sustainable?
They grow faster than hardwoods, so trees can be replanted and replaced more quickly.
Which softwood is good for outdoor use, and why?
Larch — its high sap content gives it good water resistance.
🪵 Manufactured boards
Man-made timber sheets: MDF, plywood and chipboard

🏭 What are they?

  • Manufactured boards are usually made from timber waste (fibres, chips or thin layers of wood) and adhesive (glue).
  • They are often veneered — covered with a thin layer of real wood — to make them look nicer.
  • They are cheap to buy and come in large flat sheets.

📋 Learn these three

BoardWhat it's likeHow it works & what it's used for
MDF
(medium-density fibreboard)		Smooth, light brown, can be veneeredSmooth and easy to finish. Absorbs moisture, so not for outdoor use. Used for kitchens and flat-pack furniture.
PlywoodOdd number of layers of veneer glued with the grain at 90° to each other for strength; attractive outer layerEasy to cut and finish; can be stained or painted. Used for shelving, construction and toys.
ChipboardCompacted wood chips, covered (laminated) with different coverings; cut ends are hard to finishStrong but absorbs water. Used for veneered worktops and flooring.

Why plywood is strong

Plywood: an odd number of thin layers (veneers), each with its grain at 90° to the next, glued together. That makes it strong in every direction.

👍 Good things about boards

  • Cheap
  • Come in large sheets
  • Use up timber waste
  • Can be veneered to look like real wood

👎 Watch out for

  • MDF and chipboard absorb water — not for outdoors
  • Chipboard's cut edges are hard to finish
  • The glues used can give off dust and fumes when cut — wear a dust mask

🤔 Look – cover – check

What are manufactured boards made from?
Usually timber waste (fibres, chips or thin layers) plus adhesive.
Why is plywood strong?
It is made of an odd number of layers of veneer glued with the grain of each layer at 90° to the one next to it.
Why shouldn't MDF be used for a garden bench?
MDF absorbs moisture, so it would swell and fall apart outdoors.
What is a veneer?
A thin layer of real wood glued onto a board to make it look more attractive.
💪 Material properties
The words you need to describe how a material behaves

🔬 Physical properties

The actual characteristics of the material itself.

  • Absorbency — soaks up moisture (MDF and chipboard absorb water).
  • Density — how heavy it is for its size (mass ÷ volume).
  • Thermal conductivity — lets heat through. Wood is a poor conductor, so it's good for pan handles.
  • Electrical conductivity — lets electricity through. Dry wood is a poor conductor (an insulator).

🛠️ Working properties

How a material behaves when you work or use it.

  • Strength — withstands forces (squashing, pulling, shearing).
  • Hardness — resists dents and scratches. Pine dents more easily than oak, so oak is harder.
  • Toughness — hard to break or snap; absorbs shocks (ash, beech, oak).
  • Durability — lasts a long time and resists wear and weather (oak, larch).
  • Flexibility — bends without breaking (ash).

🍳 Example — why beech for a pan handle?

A steel handle would conduct heat and burn your hand. Beech is tough and a poor conductor of heat, so it's a better choice.

🤔 Look – cover – check

What is the difference between hardness and toughness?
Hardness = resisting dents and scratches. Toughness = being hard to break or snap, absorbing shocks.
Why is wood used for the handles of cooking utensils?
Wood is a poor thermal conductor, so the handle doesn't get hot.
🔨 The tools you used
Be able to recognise each one and say what it's for

🪚 How they work together — cutting a piece of wood to length

  1. Use the try square and a pencil to mark a line at 90° across the wood.
  2. Put the bench hook over the front edge of the bench and hold the wood firmly against its top block.
  3. Cut along the waste side of the line with the tenon saw.
  4. Smooth the cut end on the disc sander, using the side of the disc that moves down towards the table.

🤔 Look – cover – check

What is the "back" of a tenon saw for?
The heavy strip of brass or steel along the top keeps the blade stiff and straight, so you get an accurate, straight cut.
What does a try square check or mark?
A right angle (90°) to a straight edge — for marking lines across wood and checking corners are square.
What is a bench hook for?
It holds the wood steady while you saw it with a tenon saw, and protects the bench top.
Give two safety rules for the disc sander.
Wear goggles; tie back long hair and loose clothing; keep fingers away from the disc; use the downward-moving side of the disc so the wood is pushed onto the table; make sure the dust extraction is on.
💻 CAD & CAM
Designing and making with computers

🖥️ CAD — Computer Aided Design

Using software to create accurate digital drawings and 3D models of products, so designs can be seen, changed and tested before anything is made.

e.g. 2D Design, Fusion 360, TinkerCAD, SketchUp

⚙️ CAM — Computer Aided Manufacture

Using software to control machines that make the product. CAM turns the CAD design into instructions for CNC machines.

e.g. laser cutter, 3D printer, CNC router, milling machine

🔤 CNC stands for Computer Numerical Control — a machine that is controlled by a computer program.

🔁 The process

CAD

  1. Sketch the first ideas.
  2. Build a digital model in CAD software, adding dimensions and features.
  3. Run simulations to test the design and spot problems.
  4. The software produces technical drawings for manufacture.

CAM

  1. Import the CAD model into CAM software.
  2. The software works out how the CNC machine will make the part.
  3. The machine cuts (or prints) the part.
  4. Check the parts are good quality and match the design.

⚖️ Advantages and disadvantages

👍 CAD

  • Very accurate — fewer mistakes than drawing by hand
  • Designs can be made and changed quickly
  • You can see the design in 3D
  • Designs can be tested on screen and shared/emailed easily

👎 CAD

  • Software and computers can be expensive
  • Takes training to learn
  • Problems if the computer crashes or files are lost

👍 CAM

  • Accurate and consistent — every product is identical
  • Fast, and machines can run for long periods
  • Fewer human errors
  • Can make complex shapes

👎 CAM

  • Machines are expensive to buy and maintain
  • Staff need training
  • If a machine breaks down, production stops
  • Can mean fewer jobs for people

🔦 Laser cutter — the CAM machine you're most likely to use

  • A high-powered laser beam cuts, etches or engraves a material, following a CAD drawing.
  • Used on thin sheets like MDF, plywood, acrylic and card.
  • Safety: never leave it running unattended, keep the lid shut, and make sure the fume extraction is on.

🤔 Look – cover – check

What do CAD and CAM stand for?
Computer Aided Design and Computer Aided Manufacture.
Name two CAM machines.
Any two of: laser cutter, 3D printer, CNC router, CNC milling machine, vinyl cutter.
Give one advantage and one disadvantage of CAM.
Advantage: accurate, consistent (identical products), fast. Disadvantage: machines are expensive, need training, production stops if a machine breaks.
What does CNC stand for?
Computer Numerical Control.
🔎 ACCESS FM
A checklist for analysing a product or writing a specification (a list of requirements for a design). You'll be given the sheet — just get familiar with the words.

♻️ The 6 Rs (part of Environment)

Recycle · Reuse · Repair · Rethink · Reduce · Refuse — ways to make a product better for the environment.

✏️ Worked example — a beech wooden spoon

A — AestheticsSmooth, pale wood with a slight pink tint and a close grain; simple curved shape.
C — CostCheap to buy (a few pounds); quick to make from a small piece of wood.
C — CustomerAnyone who cooks — adults and families.
E — EnvironmentWood is natural and biodegradable; it lasts a long time, so it can be reused instead of thrown away.
S — SizeAbout 300 mm long so your hand stays away from the hot pan; the handle fits comfortably in a hand.
S — SafetySmooth finish with no splinters; doesn't get hot; don't leave it in a pan on the heat.
F — FunctionStirs and serves food; won't scratch non-stick pans.
M — MaterialBeech (a hardwood) — tough, durable, smooth to finish and a poor conductor of heat. Shaped by cutting and sanding, or turned on a lathe.

To help write a specification (a list of requirements for a design) and to analyse and describe an existing product.
